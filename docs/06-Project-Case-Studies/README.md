# Overview

The Project Case Studies are the centerpiece of the portfolio.

Unlike traditional portfolio projects, these case studies focus on engineering thinking rather than feature lists.

Every project should explain:

- The business problem
- The engineering challenges
- The architectural decisions
- The trade-offs
- The implementation strategy
- The measurable impact

The objective is to demonstrate how problems were solved, not simply what technologies were used.

Every case study should feel like an engineering article written by the person who designed and built the product.

---

# Objective

Answer one question:

"How does Aakash solve real engineering problems?"

Visitors should finish every case study believing:

• He understands products.

• He designs scalable systems.

• He thinks before coding.

• He optimizes for users.

• He makes thoughtful technical decisions.

# Philosophy

Projects are not demonstrations of technology.

Projects are demonstrations of engineering.

Instead of saying:

"I built a social feed."

Explain:

"How I designed a scalable feed architecture capable of synchronizing cached and live content without visual instability."

The difference is storytelling through engineering.

---

# Writing Style

Every case study should be:

✓ Honest

✓ Technical

✓ Business focused

✓ Easy to read

✓ Structured

✓ Visual

Avoid:

• Buzzwords

• Marketing language

• Generic descriptions

• Technology dumping

Always explain why decisions were made.

---

# Metrics

Where possible, include measurable outcomes.

Examples:

• Faster loading time

• Reduced API requests

• Improved scroll performance

• Better caching efficiency

• Reduced rebuilds

• Higher user engagement

• Lower crash rate

Never invent metrics.

Only use values that can be supported or reasonably described.

# Success Criteria

A successful case study should answer:

✓ What was built?

✓ Why was it built?

✓ What challenges existed?

✓ How were they solved?

✓ What engineering decisions mattered?

✓ What business value was created?

✓ What was learned?

Visitors should finish reading with a clear understanding of both the product and the engineer behind it.

---

# Final Principle

A great portfolio project doesn't say:

"I built this."

It says:

"Here's how I approached a complex problem, the decisions I made, the trade-offs I considered, and the outcome those decisions created."

That is what transforms a project into an engineering case study.
